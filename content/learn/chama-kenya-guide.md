A **chama** (Swahili for "group") is a savings and investment circle, most associated with Kenya but found across East Africa. Millions of people belong to one. Some are five friends doing a monthly [merry-go-round](/learn/what-is-a-merry-go-round); others are formal investment clubs owning land and businesses.

## The three kinds of chama

| Type | What it does | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Merry-go-round** | Everyone contributes; each round one member takes the pot | Getting lump sums for school fees, stock, rent |
| **Investment** | Contributions are pooled and invested (land, shares, a business) | Building shared wealth over years |
| **Welfare** | A fund that pays members during bereavement, illness or emergencies | Security and support |

Many chamas mix them: a monthly merry-go-round, plus a slice of each contribution into a shared investment or welfare pot.

## Starting a chama in six steps

### 1. Choose members carefully
The strongest chamas are made of people who already trust each other — colleagues, school mates, church members. Ten to twenty is a practical size.

### 2. Agree the purpose
Write one sentence everyone agrees on: *"We save KES 2,000 a month, rotate KES 10,000 of it, and invest the rest in land."* A shared purpose makes later decisions easier.

### 3. Elect officials
At minimum a **chairperson**, **secretary** and **treasurer**. Larger chamas add a **loans or credit committee** and an **auditor**. Our guide to [treasurers and committees](/learn/treasurer-roles-and-committees) explains each role and how to separate duties.

### 4. Write a constitution
Cover contributions, meetings, penalties for late payment, how money can be spent, how officials are elected, and what happens when someone leaves. Use our [group fund constitution template](/learn/group-fund-constitution-template) as a starting point.

### 5. Set up money handling
Use a group bank or mobile money account with **two or more signatories**. Avoid holding cash with one person.

### 6. Keep records from day one
Contributions, payouts, loans and decisions. Records shared with every member are the best protection against misunderstandings — see [spreadsheets vs software for group records](/learn/spreadsheet-vs-software-group-records).

## Why chamas fail — and how to avoid it

- **Unclear rules** → write the constitution before the first contribution.
- **One person controls the money** → dual signatories and shared records.
- **Loans without limits** → set clear [lending rules](/learn/how-to-run-loans-in-a-savings-group) and a credit committee.
- **Members stop paying** → agreed penalties and a process for exits; see [when a member doesn't pay](/learn/when-a-member-doesnt-pay).
- **No audit** → an annual review by someone outside the committee; use our [annual audit checklist](/learn/annual-audit-checklist-savings-group).

## A sample monthly chama meeting

1. Opening and apologies
2. Contributions received this month (treasurer)
3. Merry-go-round payout for this month
4. Loan applications and repayments (credit committee)
5. Investment update
6. Welfare matters
7. Any other business and next meeting date

Keep it to an hour. Anything needing detailed discussion goes to a committee, which reports back next month.

## Running the merry-go-round part

For the rotation, keep it simple and transparent: a recorded random draw for the order, one shared calendar, and reminders before each contribution date.
