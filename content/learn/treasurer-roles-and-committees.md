Most problems in group funds aren't caused by dishonesty but by **too much power in one pair of hands**. When the same person collects dues, keeps the only record, approves loans and moves money, even an honest mistake can go unnoticed for months. Clear roles fix that.

## The core officials

### Chairperson
Leads meetings, keeps the group focused on its purpose, and breaks deadlocks. Should **not** handle money day to day.

### Secretary
Keeps minutes, the membership register and correspondence. Records decisions, such as approved loans and constitution changes, so there's one version of what was agreed.

### Treasurer / fund manager
Runs the money: records contributions, disburses approved loans and welfare, reconciles the account, and prepares reports. It's the busiest role and the one that needs the most **checks** around it.

## The committees

### Credit committee
Reviews loan applications against the rules — standing, tenure, credit score, amount and purpose — and **recommends** approve or decline. Recommendations go to the fund manager for a final decision and disbursement.

Why split it? The committee judges the **borrower**; the manager checks the **fund can afford it**. Neither alone can push a loan through. And **nobody reviews or decides their own application**. More in [how to run loans in a savings group](/learn/how-to-run-loans-in-a-savings-group).

### Audit committee
Two or three members who hold **no other office**. They have read-only access to everything: the books, loans, welfare decisions and the audit trail. They check the records against bank or mobile money statements, spot anomalies, and report to members. See the [annual audit checklist](/learn/annual-audit-checklist-savings-group).

## A simple separation-of-duties table

| Task | Who does it | Who checks it |
|---|---|---|
| Record contributions | Treasurer | Audit committee, members (can see their own record) |
| Approve a loan | Credit committee → fund manager | Audit committee |
| Disburse a loan | Fund manager | Second signatory |
| Approve a welfare claim | Fund manager | Audit committee |
| Change fund rules | General meeting | Secretary records it |
| Reconcile the account | Treasurer | Audit committee |

## Practical safeguards

- **Two signatories** for every withdrawal.
- **Members can see their own records** at any time — the most powerful fraud control there is.
- **An audit trail** that records who changed what and when, and can't be edited.
- **Rotation** of officials every few years.
- **No self-dealing**: officials can't approve their own loans or claims.

## Role descriptions you can adapt

**Fund manager / treasurer** — records contributions and repayments; disburses approved loans and welfare; reconciles the accounts monthly; prepares reports. About 2–4 hours a month for a 50-member fund with good software.

**Credit committee member** — reviews each loan application against the lending policy within an agreed time (e.g. five days); records a recommendation and reasons.

**Audit committee member** — checks the books against statements quarterly; runs the annual audit; reports to the AGM. Holds no other office.

Writing short descriptions like these helps candidates know what they're signing up for — and makes it easier to find volunteers at the [AGM](/learn/how-to-run-an-agm).
