For many groups the welfare fund is the heart of the whole thing — it's how members show up for each other when a parent dies or a child is in hospital. Designed well, it's sustainable and fair. Designed loosely, it runs dry after one bad year.

## How group welfare works

1. Each month, a small **premium** is taken from each member's contribution and set aside in a **welfare reserve**.
2. When something happens, the member files a **claim** with supporting details.
3. An official checks it against the rules and the member's **remaining cover**, and approves a payout from the reserve.

> A welfare fund is mutual support among members, not an insurance policy. It can only pay what the reserve holds, so limits matter.

## Packages: letting members choose their cover

Many funds offer tiers. For example:

| Package | Premium / month | Funeral | Medical | Welfare | Emergency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bronze | GHS 2 | 1,500 | 1,000 | 800 | 600 |
| Silver | GHS 5 | 3,000 | 2,500 | 1,800 | 1,500 |
| Gold | GHS 10 | 6,000 | 5,000 | 4,000 | 3,000 |

Limits are usually **per member per year**. Tiers let members who want more cover pay for it, without forcing higher dues on everyone.

## Is the reserve big enough? A quick check

Estimate a typical year's claims and compare them with a year of premiums:

- 80 members: 50 Bronze, 20 Silver, 10 Gold
- Premiums per year: (50×2 + 20×5 + 10×10) × 12 = **GHS 3,600**
- Expected claims: say 3 funerals (average GHS 2,500) + 5 medical (average GHS 900) = **GHS 12,000**

In this example, premiums alone cover less than a third of expected claims. The group either needs higher premiums, lower limits, or a policy of topping up the reserve from interest income. Running this check before launching packages avoids painful surprises.

## Rules that keep welfare fair

- **Eligibility:** only members in good standing can claim. See [member standing](/learn/when-a-member-doesnt-pay).
- **Waiting period:** new members wait (e.g. three months) before claiming, to prevent joining just to claim.
- **Evidence:** proportionate proof of the event and cost.
- **Limits:** remaining cover per category is tracked automatically, so nobody has to argue about it.
- **Decision and record:** who approved it, how much, and why — visible to the audit committee.

## Keeping the reserve healthy

- Keep the welfare reserve **separate** from savings in your books. [Double-entry bookkeeping](/learn/double-entry-bookkeeping-for-savings-groups) makes this automatic.
- Review claims against premiums every year at the AGM.
- Consider directing a share of loan interest to the reserve.
- Never lend out the welfare reserve.

## Common welfare mistakes

- **Promising more than the reserve can pay.** Run the reserve check every year.
- **No waiting period**, so people join just before a known expense.
- **Vague categories.** Define what "emergency" or "welfare support" covers, with examples.
- **Case-by-case amounts.** Fixed limits per package avoid accusations of favouritism.
- **Mixing welfare money with savings or loans.** The reserve should never be lent out.
