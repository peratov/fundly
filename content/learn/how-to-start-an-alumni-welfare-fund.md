A class of 2009, a church youth group, a hometown association, a WhatsApp group of old friends — sooner or later someone says: *"We should have a fund, so we can support each other."* This guide takes you from that conversation to a fund members trust, step by step.

## Step 1: Agree the purpose

Write down, in one or two sentences, what the fund is for. Common purposes:

- **Welfare** — supporting members through bereavement, hospital bills and emergencies.
- **Savings** — helping members save regularly, with their share growing over time.
- **Lending** — giving members access to affordable loans from the group's savings.
- **Projects** — donations to your school, scholarships, reunions.

Most alumni funds combine welfare and savings, and add lending once trust is established. A clear purpose makes every later decision easier.

## Step 2: Gather founding members

Start with 10–30 people who are committed and reliable. If many live abroad, read [including diaspora members](/learn/diaspora-members-group-savings). New to the jargon? Keep the [glossary](/learn/glossary) handy. It's easier to grow a trusted core than to fix a fund full of people who stopped paying in month three.

## Step 3: Write a constitution

Your constitution is the fund's rulebook. At minimum it covers membership, dues, benefits, officials, how money can be spent, meetings, and what happens when someone leaves. We've written a [group fund constitution template](/learn/group-fund-constitution-template) you can adapt.

## Step 4: Elect officials — and separate duties

You'll need a chairperson, secretary and treasurer or fund manager. As you grow, add a **credit committee** (reviews loans) and an **audit committee** (checks the books). The golden rule: **no one person should be able to both approve and move money**. Read more in [treasurers and committees](/learn/treasurer-roles-and-committees).

## Step 5: Set monthly dues

Pick an amount nearly everyone can pay every month — even in a bad month. Consider a **minimum** with the option to contribute more. See [how to set monthly dues](/learn/how-to-set-monthly-dues) for a simple method.

## Step 6: Design welfare cover

Decide what the fund pays for and how much. Many funds use **packages** (e.g. Bronze, Silver, Gold) where a small part of each member's dues funds cover with yearly limits. Our guide to [welfare packages](/learn/welfare-packages-explained) walks through the numbers.

## Step 7: Decide on loans (optional, but powerful)

Lending lets the fund earn interest that flows back to members. It also carries the most risk. Start conservatively and follow [how to run loans in a savings group](/learn/how-to-run-loans-in-a-savings-group).

## Step 8: Set up money handling

- Open a group account with **at least two signatories**.
- Collect dues by **mobile money or bank transfer** so every payment is traceable — see [collecting dues with mobile money](/learn/collecting-dues-with-mobile-money).
- Never mix fund money with anyone's personal account.

## Step 9: Keep proper records from day one

You need to know, at any moment: who has paid, each member's savings and shares, loans outstanding, welfare paid, and cash in hand. The most reliable approach is [double-entry bookkeeping](/learn/double-entry-bookkeeping-for-savings-groups), where every movement of money is recorded twice and the books must balance. Spreadsheets work for a while; here's [when to move to software](/learn/spreadsheet-vs-software-group-records).

## Step 10: Report and audit regularly

Share a simple report with members every month or quarter, and have an [annual audit](/learn/annual-audit-checklist-savings-group) by people outside the committee. Transparency is what keeps members paying.

## A first-year checklist

- [ ] Purpose agreed and written down
- [ ] Constitution adopted
- [ ] Officials elected, duties separated
- [ ] Dues and due date set
- [ ] Welfare packages defined
- [ ] Group account with two signatories
- [ ] Records system chosen
- [ ] First quarterly report shared
- [ ] Annual audit scheduled
