In a standard merry-go-round the payout order is fixed in advance. In a **bidding merry-go-round**, the order is decided each round by auction. Members who need money now offer to take less than the full pot, and the difference is shared with everyone else. It's one of the fairest answers to the question "why should early members get an interest-free loan?" (which we cover in [is a merry-go-round worth it?](/learn/is-a-merry-go-round-worth-it)).

The model is widely used. Formal *chit funds* in India run on it, as do many rotating groups across Asia, Africa and Latin America.

## How a round works

1. Every member who hasn't yet received the pot may bid.
2. A bid is the **discount** the member is willing to give up.
3. The highest discount wins the pot for that round.
4. The discount is shared out, usually by reducing everyone's contribution that round.
5. Members who have already won keep paying but don't bid again.

In the final round only one member is left, so there is no auction; they receive the full pot.

## A worked example

Ten members each contribute KES 5,000 a month, so the full pot is KES 50,000.

In month three, two members need money urgently. One bids a KES 4,000 discount, the other KES 6,000. The higher bid wins.

- The winner receives **KES 44,000** (KES 50,000 − 6,000).
- The KES 6,000 discount is shared across all ten members: **KES 600 each**.
- So everyone, including the winner, pays **KES 4,400** that month instead of 5,000.
- Ten members × KES 4,400 = KES 44,000, exactly what the winner receives.

The winner paid KES 6,000 for early access, which is effectively interest. The members who wait receive that interest as lower contributions. Early money has a price, and late members are paid for waiting.

## Rules your group should agree

Write these into your [merry-go-round rules](/learn/merry-go-round-rules-template) before the first auction:

- **Maximum discount.** Cap bids (for example at 30% of the pot) so a desperate member can't sign away most of their money.
- **Tie-breaks.** Agree a random draw if two bids are equal.
- **Sealed or open bids.** Sealed bids (written or sent privately to the organiser) reduce pressure. Open bids are faster but can become heated.
- **Organiser fee.** If the organiser takes a commission, state it in writing. Formal chit funds cap it; among friends it's usually zero.
- **After you win.** A winner must keep paying until the cycle ends. Put winners' continued payments first in your collection routine.

## The risks to watch

**Default after winning** is the biggest risk, just as in any rotation. A member who wins early at a large discount has taken a big sum and still owes many contributions. Common protections are a guarantor, as described in our guide to [guarantors in group lending](/learn/guarantors-in-group-lending), or a rule that newer members can't bid in the first few rounds. Our guide on [when a member doesn't pay](/learn/when-a-member-doesnt-pay) covers the follow-up.

**Discounts that spiral.** In hard times many members bid at once and discounts climb. A cap protects members from themselves.

**Record-keeping.** Every round now has a different contribution amount and discount. Keep a clear table of round, winner, discount, per-member share and amount paid. A shared spreadsheet or dedicated software beats a notebook here.

## Is bidding right for your group?

Bidding suits groups where several members have urgent, competing needs and others are happy to wait in exchange for a return. It suits less well groups that value simplicity or include members who may feel pressured in an auction. A good compromise is to fix the first few positions by need and auction the rest.
