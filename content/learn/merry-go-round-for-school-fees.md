School fees arrive as big, fixed-date bills — exactly the kind of expense a [merry-go-round](/learn/what-is-a-merry-go-round) is good at. With a little planning, a group of parents can make sure most members receive a pot just before their fees are due.

## Step 1: Map the fee dates

List each member's biggest fee deadline. In many school calendars, the heaviest months are the start of the academic year and the start of each term.

| Member | Children | Biggest deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Ama | 2 | September |
| Kofi | 1 | January |
| Esi | 3 | September |
| Yaw | 1 | April |

## Step 2: Choose monthly contributions and a start date

Count back from the busiest deadline. If most members need money in September and there are 10 members, a monthly circle starting in the previous December finishes in September.

Pick an amount that makes the pot meaningful: with 10 members paying GHS 300, each pot is GHS 3,000.

## Step 3: Set the order by need

This is one case where ordering by need beats a pure random draw. Assign each member the round **just before their deadline**. Where several members need the same month:

- break ties with a random draw, recorded in the group — see [deciding the payout order](/learn/merry-go-round-payout-order);
- let the member who went last in the previous cycle choose first this time.

## Step 4: Plan for families with several children

A parent with three children in school might need two pots. Options:

- they join with **two "hands"** (paying double and receiving two pots);
- or they combine a merry-go-round with a savings fund they can borrow from — see [ROSCA vs ASCA](/learn/merry-go-round-vs-savings-group).

## Step 5: Put rules around lateness

Fee deadlines are unforgiving, so late contributions hurt more here. Agree a clear deadline and a small late fee, and name guarantors for early recipients. Our [rules template](/learn/merry-go-round-rules-template) has the wording.

## Step 6: Share the calendar

Everyone should know exactly when they pay and when they receive. Our [free planner](/tools/merry-go-round) lets you set the order, then share one link with the dates — and you can add every payout to your phone's calendar.

## A sample school-fees calendar

Ten parents, GHS 300 a month, pots of GHS 3,000, starting in December:

| Round | Month | Receives | Why then |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | December | Kofi | January term fees |
| 2 | January | Abena | January fees (tie broken by draw) |
| 3 | February | Yaw | April term |
| 4 | March | Efua | April term |
| 5 | April | Kwame | Mid-year costs |
| 6 | May | Akua | Exam fees |
| 7 | June | Kojo | Uniforms and books |
| 8 | July | Ama | September fees |
| 9 | August | Esi | September fees |
| 10 | September | Kweku | September fees (last pot, arrives on time) |

Everyone pays GHS 3,000 over the ten months and receives GHS 3,000 — but most receive it just before they need it.

## Mistakes parents make

- Starting the circle too late, so the last pots arrive after the deadline.
- Setting a contribution that competes with the fees themselves in the same month.
- Not naming a guarantor for early recipients — and school-fee months are exactly when cash is tightest.
