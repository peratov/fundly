"Double-entry" sounds like something for accountants. The idea is simple, and it's the single best protection against money quietly going missing from a group fund.

## The one idea

Every movement of money is recorded **twice**: where it came **from** and where it went **to**. The two sides must be equal. If they aren't, something's wrong — and you know immediately.

Accountants call the two sides **debit** and **credit**. You don't need to memorise which is which; you just need the right accounts and the right pairs.

## The six accounts a savings group needs

| Account | What it represents | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| **Cash** | Money in the bank and mobile money accounts | What the fund has |
| **Loans receivable** | Money members owe on loans | What the fund has |
| **Member savings** | What the fund owes members for their savings | What the fund owes |
| **Welfare reserve** | Premiums set aside for welfare claims | What the fund owes / has set aside |
| **Interest income** | Interest earned from loans | What the fund has earned |
| **Penalty income** | Late-payment penalties collected | What the fund has earned |

(Add a **write-off expense** account for loans that can't be recovered.)

## Recording everyday events

### A member contributes GHS 50 (GHS 2 goes to welfare)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Cash | 50 | |
| Member savings | | 48 |
| Welfare reserve | | 2 |

### The fund lends GHS 3,000

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Loans receivable | 3,000 | |
| Cash | | 3,000 |

### The borrower repays GHS 550 (500 principal, 50 interest)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Cash | 550 | |
| Loans receivable | | 500 |
| Interest income | | 50 |

### A welfare claim of GHS 800 is paid

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Welfare reserve | 800 | |
| Cash | | 800 |

Notice each entry balances. Always.

## The check that makes it all worthwhile

At any moment:

> **Cash + loans receivable** = **member savings + welfare reserve + income earned − write-offs**

What the fund *has* equals what it *owes* plus what it has *earned*. If that equation doesn't hold, there's an error or a gap to investigate. This is what your audit committee checks — see the [annual audit checklist](/learn/annual-audit-checklist-savings-group).

## Correcting mistakes the right way

Never erase or overwrite an entry. Post a **reversing entry** (the same lines with debits and credits swapped), then the correct one. The history stays intact, and anyone can see what was corrected and why. It's a core part of [preventing fraud](/learn/preventing-fraud-in-group-funds).

## Reports you get for free

Because every event is recorded consistently, reports fall out of the books:

- cash in and out for any period;
- loans outstanding and interest earned;
- welfare paid vs premiums collected;
- each member's savings.

## Do we need an accountant?

Not for day-to-day bookkeeping, if your system records the pairs for you. Many groups still ask an accountant to review the year-end books.

## Common mistakes

- **Recording only one side.** Cash goes up but nobody's savings do — the books no longer balance.
- **Mixing welfare premiums into savings.** Members then appear to own money that's actually set aside for claims.
- **Treating the whole repayment as principal.** Interest income disappears from reports and dividends are understated.
- **Deleting wrong entries.** Always reverse and re-enter instead.
