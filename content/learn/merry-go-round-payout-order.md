In a [merry-go-round](/learn/what-is-a-merry-go-round) everyone pays in and takes out the same total, so the **order is the only thing that isn't equal**. Going first is like an interest-free loan; going last is like saving on everyone else's behalf. That's why the order causes more arguments than anything else.

Here are the five common methods and when each one works.

## 1. Random draw (the default for most groups)

Write every name on a slip, or use a digital wheel, and draw in front of the group.

- **Pros:** Nobody can claim favouritism. Fast.
- **Cons:** Someone with an urgent need might draw last.
- **Make it trustworthy:** Do it live (in person or on a video call), and record the result immediately in the group chat. Fundly's [free planner](/tools/merry-go-round) uses a cryptographically random spin, then produces a shareable calendar so the result can't be quietly changed.

## 2. By need

The group agrees that whoever has a known big expense — school fees in September, a wedding, rent due — goes at the right time for them.

- **Pros:** Makes the circle genuinely useful.
- **Cons:** Needs honest conversation, and "need" can be exaggerated.
- **Tip:** Combine with a random draw for everyone who doesn't have a date-specific need.

## 3. By seniority or trust

Long-standing, reliable members go first; newer members go later.

- **Why it works:** The biggest risk in any merry-go-round is someone receiving the pot early and then stopping payments. Putting unproven members later protects the group.
- **Downside:** It can feel like a hierarchy. Explain the reason openly.

## 4. Bidding (with a discount)

Members who want an early turn offer to take a slightly smaller pot; the difference is shared out or kept as a group reserve.

- **Pros:** Prices the value of going early.
- **Cons:** More complicated, and it starts to look like lending with interest — which may change how your group is treated legally in some countries. Keep it simple unless everyone understands it.

## 5. Fixed rotation

Alphabetical, by birthday, or by joining date. Simple and predictable, but no fairer than random.

## Handling swaps and emergencies

Life happens. A good rule is:

> Any two members can swap turns if both agree. The organiser records the swap in the group record, and the payout calendar is updated for everyone.

Swaps don't change what anyone pays or receives in total, so they're low-risk.

## Protecting the group whatever order you choose

- Put anyone without a track record **in the second half** of the order.
- Ask early recipients to name a **guarantor** who will cover them if they stop paying.
- Agree in writing what happens if someone defaults — our guide on [members who don't pay](/learn/when-a-member-doesnt-pay) covers the options.
- Share the final order and dates with everyone so there's one version of the truth.

## Recording the draw properly

However you draw, record it immediately in a way nobody can quietly change:

- the date and method of the draw;
- the full order with each member's payout date;
- who was present.

Post it in the group chat and pin it, or use a shared link where the order is visible to everyone. When there's a disagreement months later, that record settles it in seconds.
