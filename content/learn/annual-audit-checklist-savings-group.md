An annual audit isn't about suspecting anyone. It's how a fund proves to its members that their money is where the books say it is. Use this checklist as the audit committee's agenda.

## Before you start

- [ ] Get **read-only access** to all records: contributions, loans, repayments, welfare claims, share register, journal and audit log.
- [ ] Get **statements** for every bank and mobile money account for the full year.
- [ ] Get the **minutes** of meetings where loans, claims or rule changes were approved.

## 1. Cash: does the money exist?

- [ ] Closing balance in the books equals the **bank + mobile money statements** at year end.
- [ ] Every large movement on the statements matches a recorded transaction.
- [ ] No money is held in anyone's **personal account**.

## 2. Contributions

- [ ] Total contributions in the books match deposits on the statements.
- [ ] Pick **ten random members** and check their contribution history against payment references.
- [ ] Missed and late payments are recorded correctly, and standings reflect the rules.
- [ ] Any **corrections** have a reason recorded and were made by an authorised person.

## 3. Loans

- [ ] Every loan disbursed has a **committee recommendation** and a **manager approval** on record.
- [ ] No official approved **their own** loan.
- [ ] Interest was charged at the agreed product rate — spot-check using the method in [flat vs reducing-balance interest](/learn/flat-vs-reducing-balance-interest).
- [ ] Repayments on the statements match repayments in the books.
- [ ] Overdue loans are identified; penalties were applied according to the rules.
- [ ] Any **written-off** loans were approved and recorded.

## 4. Welfare claims

- [ ] Each approved claim has supporting evidence and was within the member's **remaining limit**.
- [ ] Claimants were in good standing.
- [ ] Payouts on the statements match payouts in the books.
- [ ] The welfare reserve balance looks sustainable — see [welfare packages explained](/learn/welfare-packages-explained).

## 5. Shares and member balances

- [ ] Total member savings in the books equals the sum of individual member balances.
- [ ] Shares were issued at the correct price for each period.
- [ ] Any **bonus share grants** were approved and recorded.

## 6. The books balance

- [ ] Total **debits equal total credits** in the journal.
- [ ] Assets (cash + loans receivable) equal savings + welfare reserve + retained income. See [double-entry bookkeeping for savings groups](/learn/double-entry-bookkeeping-for-savings-groups).

## 7. Controls

- [ ] Two signatories were required for withdrawals.
- [ ] Roles were separated — see [treasurers and committees](/learn/treasurer-roles-and-committees).
- [ ] The audit log shows no unexplained edits or deletions.

## 8. Report to members

Write a one-page summary for the AGM: opening and closing cash, contributions received, loans disbursed and repaid, welfare paid, income earned, any issues found and recommendations. Share it with every member.

---

## Common findings — and what they mean

| Finding | Usually means | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Cash in books ≠ statement | Unrecorded payment, reversal or fee | Trace and post a correcting entry |
| Contributions without payment references | Cash handling | Move to traceable payments |
| Loan approved without committee notes | Process skipped | Reinforce the two-step approval |
| Welfare over the member's limit | Limits not tracked | Track remaining cover per member |
| Totals don't add up across reports | Spreadsheet formula errors | Move to double-entry books |

An audit that finds small issues and fixes them is a success, not a failure. Report findings calmly, with recommendations and a date for follow-up.
