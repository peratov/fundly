Every group fund starts somewhere — usually a notebook or a spreadsheet the treasurer keeps. That's fine at first. The question is when it stops being fine.

## Option 1: A notebook

**Good:** zero cost, works offline, familiar.
**Bad:** one copy, no backups, members can't see their records without asking, and totals are recalculated by hand.

A notebook suits a small [merry-go-round](/learn/what-is-a-merry-go-round) where you only track who paid each round.

## Option 2: Excel or Google Sheets

**Good:** free or cheap, flexible, formulas do the arithmetic, Google Sheets can be shared.

**Bad, once the fund grows:**

- **Formulas break silently.** One pasted value over a formula and the totals are wrong for months.
- **No audit trail.** You can't easily see who changed a number or why.
- **Everything is in one file.** Either everyone can edit everything, or members can't see their own records.
- **Loans get complicated.** Interest, repayment schedules, arrears and penalties across many members quickly outgrow a sheet.
- **It depends on one person.** When the treasurer changes, the knowledge often leaves with them.

## Option 3: Purpose-built software

**Good:** members see their own balances, loans and welfare cover; payments by mobile money post automatically; loans, interest and standing are calculated consistently; there's an audit trail; roles control who can do what; the books balance by design.

**Bad:** a monthly cost, and a small learning curve.

## Side-by-side

| | Notebook | Spreadsheet | Software |
|---|---|---|---|
| Members see own records | ✗ | Partly | ✓ |
| Audit trail of changes | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ |
| Loan schedules and arrears | Manual | Fragile | Automatic |
| Welfare limits enforced | Manual | Manual | Automatic |
| Mobile money reconciliation | Manual | Manual | Automatic |
| Works when treasurer changes | Risky | Risky | ✓ |
| Cost | Free | Free | Subscription |

## Signs it's time to switch

- You have **more than about 30 members**, or more than one person updating records.
- You've started **lending**, and interest or arrears calculations cause disagreements.
- Members keep asking the treasurer **"what's my balance?"**
- The year-end audit takes **weeks**.
- You've found a **mistake** that had been sitting in the sheet for months.

## How to switch without losing history

1. **Clean the member list** — one row per member, with a unique number and joining date.
2. **Export contributions** as rows: member number, month, amount, date paid.
3. **Import and validate.** Good software checks every row and reports problems *before* saving anything.
4. **Reconcile** the opening balances with your bank or mobile money statement.
5. **Run both in parallel for one month**, then retire the spreadsheet.

## If you're staying on a spreadsheet for now

Make it as safe as possible:

- **One tab per purpose** — members, contributions (one row per member per month), loans, repayments, welfare — rather than one giant sheet.
- **Lock formula cells** and protect the sheet so only the treasurer can edit.
- **Keep dated backups** every month.
- **Never type over a formula** with a number; if a figure is wrong, fix the input.
- **Reconcile monthly** against statements, and write down who checked it.
- **Share a read-only summary** with members every month.
