A receipt is the fund's confirmation that it received money from a member. It protects both sides. The member can prove they paid, and the treasurer can prove what came in. When receipts are missing or informal, disputes follow. Here's how to do receipts properly without adding hours of work.

## What a receipt must show

Every receipt should include:

1. **a unique, sequential receipt number** (e.g. R-2026-0142);
2. **date received**;
3. **member name and member number**;
4. **amount**, in figures and currency;
5. **what it's for**: dues for which month, loan repayment for which loan, fine, entry fee;
6. **payment method and reference**, such as the mobile money transaction ID or bank reference;
7. **issued by**: the treasurer's name, or "system generated".

Sequential numbering matters. A gap in the sequence shows that a receipt is missing and needs explaining. That simple check is one of the controls in [preventing fraud in group funds](/learn/preventing-fraud-in-group-funds).

## Why a screenshot isn't a receipt

Members often send a screenshot of their mobile money confirmation and consider that proof. There are two problems:

- **It proves they sent something, not that the fund received it.** Money can go to the wrong number or fail.
- **Screenshots and SMS messages can be faked.** Fake payment messages are a common scam against traders and treasurers alike.

The only reliable proof is the payment appearing on **the fund's own statement**. Treat screenshots as a pointer, then confirm on the statement during [reconciliation](/learn/reconciling-mobile-money-statements) before issuing the receipt.

## Issuing receipts

**Paper receipt book (duplicate).** Works for cash meetings. One copy goes to the member and one stays in the book. Never tear out a page; void mistakes and keep them.

**Digital receipts.** Faster and harder to lose. A receipt goes to the member by SMS, WhatsApp or email once the payment is confirmed. Fund software can do this automatically the moment a payment lands, so the treasurer never types one.

**For cash**, always issue a receipt at the moment of handover, and follow the routine in [handling cash contributions safely](/learn/handling-cash-contributions-safely).

## Receipts for money going out

The fund should also keep proof for every payment out:

- **loans:** the approval, the signed agreement and the transfer reference;
- **welfare:** the committee decision and evidence of the event;
- **expenses:** the supplier's invoice or receipt.

Your auditor will ask for these; see the [annual audit checklist](/learn/annual-audit-checklist-savings-group).

## Storing records

- keep receipts in numbered order, by year;
- store digital copies in a shared folder or system with access limited to officers;
- keep backups, because one lost phone shouldn't mean lost records;
- restrict who can see members' details (see [member data privacy](/learn/member-data-privacy-for-group-funds)).

## When a member disputes a payment

1. Ask for the transaction ID, date and paying number.
2. Search the fund's statement for that reference.
3. If found, record it, issue a receipt and apologise for the delay.
4. If not found, show the member the statement extract and ask them to check with their provider.

Most disputes end at step 3, usually a payment from a different number. Our guide to [resolving disputes in group funds](/learn/resolving-disputes-in-group-funds) covers what to do if it doesn't.
