How often your [merry-go-round](/learn/what-is-a-merry-go-round) pays out shapes everything: the size of each contribution, how long the circle lasts, and how easy it is for members to keep up.

## Match the schedule to how members earn

| Members mostly… | Best fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Earn a monthly salary | **Monthly**, a few days after payday | Contribution fits the pay cycle |
| Are paid fortnightly | **Every two weeks** | Same logic |
| Trade or earn daily/weekly | **Weekly** | Smaller, frequent amounts are easier than one large one |

A schedule that doesn't match income is the most common cause of late payments.

## Group size × frequency = how long you wait

The circle lasts one round per member. The last person waits the full length.

| Members | Weekly | Every 2 weeks | Monthly |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 5 weeks | 10 weeks | 5 months |
| 10 | 10 weeks | 20 weeks | 10 months |
| 20 | 20 weeks | 40 weeks | 20 months |

With 20 members on a monthly schedule, the last person waits well over a year and a half — a long time to trust that everyone keeps paying. Large groups often suit a faster schedule, or splitting into two smaller circles.

## Match the pot to the goal

Work backwards from what members want to buy:

- **Stock for a small business** — often a weekly or fortnightly pot among traders.
- **Rent or school fees** — monthly, timed to deadlines. See [merry-go-rounds for school fees](/learn/merry-go-round-for-school-fees).
- **A big purchase** — monthly with a larger contribution.

Pot = contribution × number of members. Ten members at GHS 100 a week make a GHS 1,000 weekly pot; the same group at GHS 400 a month makes a GHS 4,000 monthly pot.

## Practical tips

- Pick a **fixed day and time** ("Friday by 6 pm", "the 28th by noon").
- For weekly circles, keep amounts small enough that one bad week doesn't cause a default.
- For monthly circles, avoid the last days of the month if salaries arrive late.
- Whatever you choose, [write it into your rules](/learn/merry-go-round-rules-template).

## A worked comparison

Imagine 8 friends who can each put aside about GHS 400 a month.

| Option | Contribution | Pot | Circle length | Last payout |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly | GHS 100 | GHS 800 | 8 weeks | Week 8 |
| Fortnightly | GHS 200 | GHS 1,600 | 16 weeks | Week 16 |
| Monthly | GHS 400 | GHS 3,200 | 8 months | Month 8 |

All three move the same money over time, but they feel very different. The weekly circle finishes in two months with small pots — great for restocking a shop. The monthly one produces pots big enough for rent or fees, but the last member waits eight months.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing weekly because it sounds faster** when members are salaried — contributions end up late every week before payday.
- **Very long circles.** Past about 12 rounds, the risk that someone's circumstances change grows quickly. Split big groups into two circles.
- **Skipping holidays without a rule.** Decide in advance whether December or festive weeks are skipped, and shift the calendar for everyone if so.
- **Forgetting the calendar.** A shared calendar of payout dates prevents most "I thought it was next week" disputes.
