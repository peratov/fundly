Every fund eventually has a dispute. A member insists they paid, a loan decision seems unfair, or someone suspects the treasurer. Disputes themselves don't break groups; handling them badly does. Here is a process your group can adopt before the first one happens.

## Start with the most common causes

Most disputes fall into a few categories:

- **"I paid, but it's not recorded."** A missing or misallocated payment.
- **"Why did they get a loan and I didn't?"** A perceived unfair decision.
- **"Where did the money go?"** A lack of reporting.
- **"That's not what we agreed."** Unwritten or unclear rules.
- **Conflicts of interest**, such as an officer approving a relative's loan.

Notice that most of these are prevented by good records, written rules and regular reporting. A clear [monthly financial report](/learn/monthly-financial-report-for-members) prevents many disputes before they start.

## The dispute ladder

Use the lowest step that resolves the issue.

**Step 1: Check the facts.** Before any discussion, pull the records: the payment history, the mobile money or bank statement, the minutes and the relevant rule. Many disputes end here. A member's payment might be found under a different phone number, as described in [reconciling mobile money statements](/learn/reconciling-mobile-money-statements).

**Step 2: Talk privately.** The relevant officer and the member speak directly, in private, within a few days. Listen first. Explain what the records show and which rule applies.

**Step 3: Committee review.** If it's still unresolved, the member submits the complaint in writing to the committee, which decides within a set time (for example 14 days) and replies in writing with reasons.

**Step 4: Mediation.** Two respected members not involved in the matter meet both sides and try to reach an agreement. This works well for personal disputes that numbers alone won't solve.

**Step 5: General meeting.** For serious matters, such as removing an officer or writing off a large amount, the membership decides by vote. Follow the procedure in [how to run an AGM](/learn/how-to-run-an-agm) or call a special general meeting.

**Step 6: External help.** For suspected theft, or where the group is registered and rules require it, involve the police, the registrar or a lawyer.

## Conflict-of-interest rules

Adopt three simple rules and write them into your [constitution](/learn/group-fund-constitution-template):

1. **Declare.** Any officer with a personal interest in a decision (a relative's loan, a business supplying the fund) must say so.
2. **Step out.** That officer leaves the discussion and doesn't vote.
3. **Minute it.** The declaration and the step-out are recorded in the minutes.

## Keep it fair and calm

- Apply rules the same way to everyone, including officers and their friends.
- Give reasons in writing; "the committee decided" is not a reason.
- Set time limits so issues don't drag on for months.
- Separate the person from the problem: "the payment of 12 March is missing", not "you're lying".

## Handling suspicions about officers

If members suspect misuse of funds, move quickly but carefully. Ask for an independent review of the records, temporarily require an extra signatory for withdrawals, and avoid public accusations until the facts are clear. Our guide to [preventing fraud in group funds](/learn/preventing-fraud-in-group-funds) lists the warning signs and controls.

## After the dispute

Once it's resolved, ask what rule or process would have prevented it and fix that. Sometimes the fix is a clearer exit rule (see [member joining and exit policy](/learn/member-joining-and-exit-policy)). Sometimes it's a better reminder routine or a loan policy that's published to everyone. And if members drifted away during the conflict, see [how to revive inactive members](/learn/reviving-inactive-members).
