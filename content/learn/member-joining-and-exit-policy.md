Joining and leaving are when most money disputes happen. New members wonder why they can't claim straight away. Departing members expect everything back. Families of a deceased member don't know what they're entitled to. A short written policy, agreed before anyone needs it, prevents almost all of that.

## Joining: the four decisions

**1. Who can join.** For an alumni fund, it might be any graduate of the class or school. For a community fund, perhaps existing members must vouch for applicants. Write down the eligibility test and who approves applications.

**2. Entry fee.** A one-off fee (for example one month's dues) covers admin and is fair to members whose contributions built the reserves. Some funds instead ask new members to "buy in" to the current share value, which is covered in [shares and dividends explained](/learn/shares-and-dividends-explained).

**3. Waiting periods.** Set a waiting period before new members can:

- claim welfare benefits, commonly 3–6 months of paid-up dues;
- borrow, commonly 3–6 months of savings history, so the fund can assess them (see [fair credit scoring for members](/learn/fair-credit-scoring-for-members)).

Waiting periods protect the fund against people joining just before an expected claim.

**4. Onboarding.** Give every new member the constitution, the dues schedule, how to pay and whom to contact. Capture only the personal data you need, as explained in [member data privacy](/learn/member-data-privacy-for-group-funds), including a **nominee** (next of kin) for benefits.

## Leaving: voluntary exits

A clear exit rule typically says:

- **Notice.** A member gives written notice, often one month.
- **What's refunded.** Their savings or share balance at current value.
- **What's deducted.** Any outstanding loan principal and interest, unpaid dues and fines.
- **What isn't refunded.** Welfare contributions, which bought cover the member already enjoyed, and entry fees.
- **When.** Within a set period (e.g. 30–60 days), so the fund can arrange cash without forcing a loan recall.
- **Guarantees.** A member who guarantees someone else's loan can't leave until that loan is repaid or a new guarantor steps in. See [guarantors in group lending](/learn/guarantors-in-group-lending).

**Worked example.** Ama has savings of GHS 4,200 and an outstanding loan of GHS 1,500 with GHS 60 interest due, plus two months' unpaid dues of GHS 100 each. Her exit payment is 4,200 − 1,500 − 60 − 200 = **GHS 2,440**.

## Leaving: involuntary exits

Your constitution should say when a member can be removed, for example after six months of non-payment or after fraud, and who decides. Removal should follow a fair process: written notice, a chance to respond, then a decision by the committee or members. Our guide to [resolving disputes in group funds](/learn/resolving-disputes-in-group-funds) outlines a fair process.

## When a member dies

This is the hardest case, so decide it in advance:

- the fund pays the **nominee** the member's savings balance minus any loan, plus any death benefit under the welfare scheme (see [welfare packages explained](/learn/welfare-packages-explained));
- outstanding loans may be written off up to a limit, or recovered from savings first and then from guarantors, depending on your rules;
- the fund asks for a death certificate or a letter from the family and the nominee's ID before paying.

Keep nominee details up to date; ask members to confirm them at every AGM.

## Put it in the constitution

Add these rules to your [group fund constitution](/learn/group-fund-constitution-template) and share them with every new member. When the policy is written and applied equally, exits stop being personal. They become a calculation everyone can check.
