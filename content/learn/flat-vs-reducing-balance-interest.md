Two loans can both say "12% a year" and cost very different amounts. The difference is whether interest is **flat** or on a **reducing balance**. Whichever your group uses, members should understand it.

## Flat interest

Interest is calculated on the **original amount** for the whole term:

> Interest = principal × annual rate × (months ÷ 12)

It doesn't matter that the borrower is paying the loan down each month — interest is always based on the starting amount.

## Reducing-balance interest

Interest is calculated each month on **what's still owed**. As the balance falls, so does the interest. Banks and most regulated lenders use this method.

## Worked example: GHS 1,200 at 12% for 12 months

### Flat

- Interest = 1,200 × 12% × 1 = **GHS 144**
- Total repaid = **GHS 1,344**
- Monthly instalment = 1,344 ÷ 12 = **GHS 112**

### Reducing balance (1% a month)

- Monthly instalment ≈ **GHS 106.62**
- Total repaid ≈ **GHS 1,279.43**
- Interest ≈ **GHS 79.43**

| | Flat 12% | Reducing 12% |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly payment | GHS 112.00 | GHS 106.62 |
| Total interest | GHS 144.00 | GHS 79.43 |
| Equivalent reducing-balance rate | about 21.6% a year | 12% a year |

A flat 12% costs roughly what a reducing-balance **21–22%** would. That isn't a trick — it's arithmetic — but members should know it.

## Which should your group use?

| | Flat | Reducing balance |
|---|---|---|
| Easy to explain | ✓✓ | ✓ |
| Easy to calculate by hand | ✓✓ | ✗ (needs a formula) |
| Same instalment every month | ✓ | ✓ (with amortising schedule) |
| Rewards early repayment | Only if you allow it | ✓ |
| Matches bank rates | ✗ | ✓ |

**Flat** suits most informal groups: everyone can check the numbers, and schedules are simple. If you use it, consider:

- setting **lower headline rates** than a bank would quote;
- stating the approximate equivalent rate when you explain products;
- deciding what happens on **early repayment** — for example, charging interest only for the months used.

**Reducing balance** suits larger funds with software that can produce schedules, especially if members compare against bank loans.

## Whatever you choose

- Fix the method in your [constitution](/learn/group-fund-constitution-template) and lending policy.
- Show every borrower the **full schedule** before they accept.
- Split each repayment correctly between principal and interest in the books — see [how to run loans in a savings group](/learn/how-to-run-loans-in-a-savings-group).

## Early repayment: the question members always ask

Suppose Ama borrows GHS 1,200 at a flat 12% for 12 months and wants to repay everything after 6 months.

- **Strict flat interest:** the full GHS 144 interest is due, even though she only borrowed for half the term. Simple, but it feels unfair and discourages early repayment.
- **Pro-rated flat interest:** charge interest only for the months used — GHS 72 — plus whatever principal is still owed.
- **Reducing balance:** interest naturally stops accruing once the balance is repaid.

Whichever you choose, write it into your lending policy so the answer is the same for everyone.

## Explaining the rate to members

A one-line disclosure avoids misunderstandings:

> *"Personal loans cost 10% flat per year. On a 6-month GHS 1,000 loan that's GHS 50 in interest, repaid as 6 instalments of GHS 175."*

Showing the real money amounts, not just a percentage, is the clearest way to be fair.
