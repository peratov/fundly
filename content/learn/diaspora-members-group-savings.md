Many alumni associations and hometown groups have a large share of members living abroad. They're often among the most committed contributors — but only if the group makes it easy and keeps them fully informed.

## Keep one fund currency

Mixing currencies in one set of books causes endless confusion. Pick the **home currency** as the fund's currency and record every contribution in it. Members abroad send money home in that currency, either:

- via a **remittance service** that pays out to the group's mobile money or bank account; or
- through a **trusted relative** who pays locally on their behalf (record the payment against the member, not the relative).

Exchange rates and transfer fees are the member's cost; the fund records what arrives.

## Make paying effortless from abroad

- Share the group's **account details** clearly and confirm any changes in two places — scams often target diaspora members. See [collecting dues with mobile money](/learn/collecting-dues-with-mobile-money).
- Allow **paying several months in advance**, which suits people who send money home periodically.
- Agree what happens if a transfer arrives a few days late because of processing times.

## Transparency matters more at a distance

Members abroad can't drop by the treasurer's house to ask about their balance. Give every member **online access to their own record**: contributions, savings, loans and welfare cover. It's the single biggest trust-builder for diaspora members — see [spreadsheet vs software](/learn/spreadsheet-vs-software-group-records).

## Time zones and meetings

- Schedule meetings at times that work across zones (a weekend afternoon at home is often evening in Europe and morning in North America).
- Allow **online attendance** and, if your [constitution](/learn/group-fund-constitution-template) permits, **proxy votes**.
- Send AGM papers at least two weeks ahead — see [how to run an AGM](/learn/how-to-run-an-agm).

## Welfare and loans for members abroad

- Welfare often supports **family at home** — for example a parent's funeral. Be clear in your rules about who is covered.
- Lending to members abroad can work, but recovery is harder if things go wrong. Many groups set lower limits or require a guarantor at home — see [how to run loans](/learn/how-to-run-loans-in-a-savings-group).

## A fair deal for everyone

Diaspora members sometimes feel they pay in but have less say; home members sometimes feel diaspora members have it easy. Equal access to information, equal voting rights and the same rules for everyone keep the group together.

## A checklist for diaspora-friendly groups

- [ ] One fund currency, stated in the constitution
- [ ] Clear, verified payment details — changes confirmed in two places
- [ ] Advance payments allowed and recorded against the right months
- [ ] Every member can see their own balance online
- [ ] Meetings at times that work across time zones, with online attendance
- [ ] Proxy or online voting permitted
- [ ] Welfare rules state which family members at home are covered
- [ ] Loan rules for members abroad (limits or a guarantor at home)
