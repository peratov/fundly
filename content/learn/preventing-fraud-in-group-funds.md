Most groups never experience fraud. But when it happens, it's usually because the fund relied on trust **instead of** controls, rather than trust **plus** controls. These twelve controls cost almost nothing and protect everyone — including honest officials, who deserve protection from suspicion.

## Money handling

**1. Never use personal accounts.** Group money lives in a group bank or mobile money account, never in the treasurer's personal account.

**2. Two signatories for every withdrawal.** No single person can move money alone.

**3. Traceable payments only.** Collect dues by [mobile money or bank transfer](/learn/collecting-dues-with-mobile-money), not cash handed over at meetings. Every payment gets a reference.

**4. Pay recipients directly.** Loans and welfare payouts go straight to the member's own account or wallet.

## Records

**5. Members can see their own records.** This is the most powerful control of all. If every member can check their balance, loans and contributions at any time, errors and manipulation get noticed quickly.

**6. An audit trail that can't be edited.** Every change records who made it, when, and why. Corrections are new entries, not overwrites.

**7. Double-entry books.** Every transaction recorded twice, debits equal credits. Money can't quietly disappear from one place without showing up somewhere else. See [double-entry bookkeeping for savings groups](/learn/double-entry-bookkeeping-for-savings-groups).

**8. Monthly reconciliation.** The treasurer matches the books to the statements every month; the audit committee checks it.

## Decisions

**9. Separate duties.** The person who reviews a loan isn't the person who disburses it — see [treasurers and committees](/learn/treasurer-roles-and-committees).

**10. No self-approval.** Officials can never approve their own loans, welfare claims or bonus shares.

**11. Rules applied the same way to everyone.** Loan limits, credit scores and welfare limits calculated from the rules, not negotiated — see [fair credit scoring](/learn/fair-credit-scoring-for-members).

## Oversight

**12. An independent annual audit.** By people with no other office. Use our [annual audit checklist](/learn/annual-audit-checklist-savings-group).

## Warning signs

- The treasurer is reluctant to share records or statements.
- Reports are always "coming next month".
- Loans to officials or their relatives are unusually frequent or large.
- Cash collections are common and receipts are rare.
- Numbers in reports don't match what members see.

None of these prove wrongdoing, but each deserves a calm question.

## If you suspect a problem

1. Stay factual; avoid public accusations.
2. Pause non-essential withdrawals (with two signatories, this is straightforward).
3. Ask the audit committee to reconcile records against statements.
4. Take legal advice before any formal action.

## Honest mistakes look a lot like fraud

Most discrepancies aren't theft. Typical innocent causes:

- a payment recorded against the wrong member or month;
- a mobile money reversal nobody recorded;
- a loan repayment recorded twice;
- a welfare payout made in cash and never entered.

That's why the controls above protect honest officials too: with traceable payments, an audit trail and monthly reconciliation, a mistake is found in days and fixed with a correcting entry — instead of surfacing a year later as a suspicion nobody can disprove.
