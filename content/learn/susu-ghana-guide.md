**Susu** is one of Ghana's most familiar ways to save. Traders, civil servants, church members and old school mates all use it, and the word covers two related arrangements that are worth separating.

## Two kinds of susu

### 1. The susu collector

A collector (sometimes called a *susu man* or *susu woman*) visits market traders and small businesses to collect a fixed daily or weekly amount. At the end of the cycle — often a month — each saver gets back their total **minus one contribution**, which is the collector's fee.

It works as a safe, disciplined place to put small daily earnings. The risk is the collector: if they disappear, savings go with them. Many collectors now operate through registered microfinance companies, which is safer than an informal arrangement.

### 2. The rotating susu group

This is the classic [merry-go-round](/learn/what-is-a-merry-go-round): a group of friends or colleagues each contribute the same amount on the same schedule, and each round one member takes the whole pot. There's no fee and no collector — just the group.

The rest of this guide is about running a rotating susu group well.

## Setting up a rotating susu

| Decision | Common choice | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Group size | 6–15 people | Only people you'd lend money to directly |
| Contribution | GHS 50–500 per round | Pick what everyone can pay in a bad month |
| Frequency | Monthly, around payday | Weekly suits traders with daily income |
| Payout day | Last Friday of the month | Give a clear cut-off time, e.g. 6 pm |
| Order | Random draw | See [deciding the payout order](/learn/merry-go-round-payout-order) |

## Paying with mobile money

Mobile money has made susu much easier to run:

- Members send their contribution **directly to the recipient's MoMo number**, so no one person holds everyone's money.
- Every transfer has a reference, which settles "I already paid" arguments.
- Ask everyone to post a screenshot or reference in the group chat, and have the organiser tick them off.

Our guide to [collecting dues with mobile money](/learn/collecting-dues-with-mobile-money) covers references, reconciliation and avoiding scams.

## Keeping your susu safe

1. **Write the rules down.** A one-page agreement signed (or at least acknowledged in the group chat) by everyone. Start from our [rules template](/learn/merry-go-round-rules-template).
2. **Protect the early pots.** New members go later in the order; early recipients name a guarantor.
3. **Keep one record everyone can see.** Who's paid this round, who's next, and when.
4. **Agree what happens if someone doesn't pay** before it happens — see [when a member doesn't pay](/learn/when-a-member-doesnt-pay).
5. **Be wary of "susu schemes" promising returns.** A genuine rotating susu never pays out more than was paid in. Any scheme promising you'll get back double is not a susu.

## When your susu outgrows itself

Many susu groups eventually want to do more: keep a welfare fund for funerals and hospital bills, lend to members, or save towards something together. That's the point to move from a merry-go-round to a proper group fund — see [how to start an alumni or community welfare fund](/learn/how-to-start-an-alumni-welfare-fund).

## Susu collector or susu group — which suits you?

Choose a **collector** (ideally one working with a licensed institution) if you earn small daily amounts and mainly want a safe place to put them. Choose a **rotating group** if you want a larger lump sum at a set time and have people you trust to save with.
