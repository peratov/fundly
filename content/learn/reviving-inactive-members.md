Almost every alumni and welfare fund has the same shape. There's a keen core that always pays, and a long tail of members who joined enthusiastically and then drifted. Reviving even a quarter of that tail can transform the fund. This guide is a practical playbook.

## Step 1: Measure the problem

Split your member list into three groups:

- **Active:** paid in the last 3 months;
- **Slipping:** last paid 3–12 months ago;
- **Lapsed:** no payment in over 12 months.

Most effort should go to the **slipping** group, who are the easiest to bring back. Good records make this a two-minute job; with a notebook it's a weekend. See [spreadsheet vs software for group records](/learn/spreadsheet-vs-software-group-records).

## Step 2: Find out why they stopped

Ask a handful of slipping members directly and kindly. The usual answers:

- **"I forgot."** There's no reminder routine.
- **"Paying is a hassle."** They have to find the treasurer's number and send a screenshot.
- **"I don't know where the money goes."** There's no reporting.
- **"I owe so much now."** The arrears feel impossible.
- **"I moved abroad."** There's no easy way to pay from overseas (see [diaspora members](/learn/diaspora-members-group-savings)).
- **"I was unhappy about a decision."** There's an unresolved grievance (see [resolving disputes](/learn/resolving-disputes-in-group-funds)).

## Step 3: Remove friction

- One payment method that works for everyone, ideally a group short code or payment link; see [collecting dues with mobile money](/learn/collecting-dues-with-mobile-money).
- Automatic receipts, so no screenshots are needed; see [receipts and proof of payment](/learn/receipts-and-proof-of-payment).
- Friendly, regular reminders; start with our [WhatsApp reminder templates](/learn/whatsapp-reminder-templates-for-dues).
- An option to pay several months at once.

## Step 4: Show the value

People pay for what they can see. Every month, share:

- a short [monthly financial report](/learn/monthly-financial-report-for-members) showing the balance and where money went;
- welfare support given, with the family's permission and without private details;
- loans issued and repaid (totals only);
- upcoming events or projects.

A single message saying "This month the fund supported two members' families and has GHS 48,000 in reserve" does more than ten reminders.

## Step 5: Make coming back easy

Large arrears are a wall. Consider a **time-limited return offer**, approved at a general meeting:

- waive late fines (but not dues) for members who return within 60 days;
- or cap back-payment at, say, six months of dues;
- or let returning members pay arrears in instalments alongside current dues.

Be fair to members who never stopped. Keep the offer modest, time-limited and open to everyone.

## Step 6: The re-engagement message

Personal beats broadcast. A message from a classmate or friend works best:

> Hi Kwame, it's Esi from the 2009 class fund. We've missed you! This year the fund helped four families and we've just started member loans. We're welcoming people back. Fines are waived if you restart before 30 November. Here's the payment link: [link]. Anything I can help with?

## Step 7: Keep them

Returning members usually leave again for the same reason they left the first time. Fix the cause the survey revealed, whether that's reminders, reporting or a payment method. Then follow up with a thank-you after their first payment back.

## Where the rules fit in

If some members stay inactive after all this, apply your constitution's lapsing rule so your records reflect reality. See [member joining and exit policy](/learn/member-joining-and-exit-policy).
