A **merry-go-round** is one of the oldest and simplest ways people save together. A group of friends, colleagues or neighbours agree to pay the same amount on a regular schedule — say GHS 200 every month — and each time, the whole pot goes to one member. The order rotates until everyone has had exactly one turn.

Economists call it a **rotating savings and credit association (ROSCA)**. You probably know it by a local name: **susu** or **nnoboa** in Ghana, **chama** in Kenya, **esusu** or **ajo** in Nigeria, **tontine** in francophone Africa, **stokvel** in South Africa, **committee** in parts of South Asia, **tanda** in Mexico. The mechanics are the same everywhere.

## How a merry-go-round works, step by step

1. **Agree the basics.** How much each person pays (the *contribution*), how often (weekly, fortnightly or monthly) and when the first payout happens.
2. **Decide the payout order.** Who receives the pot in round one, round two and so on. This is the single most important decision — see [how to decide the payout order fairly](/learn/merry-go-round-payout-order).
3. **Everyone pays in each round.** Contributions go to the person whose turn it is (or to an organiser who passes it on).
4. **One person takes the pot.** With 10 members paying GHS 200, the pot is GHS 2,000.
5. **Repeat until everyone has had a turn.** After 10 rounds the circle is complete. Many groups immediately start another.

### A worked example

Ten colleagues each contribute GHS 200 on the last Friday of every month.

| Round | Month | Receives | Pot |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | January | Ama | GHS 2,000 |
| 2 | February | Kofi | GHS 2,000 |
| 3 | March | Esi | GHS 2,000 |
| … | … | … | … |
| 10 | October | Yaw | GHS 2,000 |

By October, each person has paid in GHS 2,000 and received GHS 2,000. Nobody gains or loses money — but Ama had GHS 2,000 in January, something she could only have saved alone by October.

## Why people love merry-go-rounds

- **Early access to a lump sum.** Everyone except the last person gets their money sooner than saving alone. For the first person it's effectively an interest-free loan from the group.
- **Commitment and discipline.** It's much harder to skip a payment your friends are counting on than to skip a transfer to your own savings account.
- **No bank, no paperwork, no fees.** A shared WhatsApp group and a notebook are enough to start.
- **Social glue.** Many circles double as a monthly get-together.

## The risks — and how good groups manage them

| Risk | What it looks like | How to reduce it |
|---|---|---|
| **Default** | Someone who has already received the pot stops paying | Put newer or less-known members later in the order; ask for a guarantor; agree penalties in writing |
| **Disputes about order** | "Why does she always go first?" | Use a transparent random draw and record it — our [free planner](/tools/merry-go-round) spins a wheel in front of everyone |
| **Lost records** | Nobody is sure who paid in March | Keep one shared record everyone can see |
| **Organiser misuse** | Money is collected but not passed on | Pay the recipient directly by mobile money, or rotate the organiser role |
| **Emergencies** | A member needs money before their turn | Allow swaps if both members agree, and record them |

Most problems come from unclear rules rather than bad people. Writing down a one-page agreement before the first round prevents almost every argument. We've put together a [merry-go-round rules template](/learn/merry-go-round-rules-template) you can copy.

## Merry-go-round vs a savings group

A merry-go-round moves money *between* members; it doesn't build a shared pot. If your group wants to lend to each other, earn interest, or keep a welfare fund for funerals and hospital bills, you need an **accumulating savings group** instead. See [merry-go-round vs savings group](/learn/merry-go-round-vs-savings-group) for when to switch.

## How to start one this week

1. Message 5–15 people you trust and agree the amount and frequency — see [weekly, fortnightly or monthly](/learn/weekly-vs-monthly-merry-go-round) for choosing the schedule.
2. Pick a start date that suits when most people get paid.
3. Do a fair, recorded draw for the order.
4. Share the rules and the payout calendar with everyone.
5. Remind people a few days before each payment — [these WhatsApp templates](/learn/whatsapp-reminder-templates-for-dues) help.
