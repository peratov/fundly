Every group eventually has a member who pays late or stops paying. How you respond decides whether the group survives. The good news: a clear, consistent process solves most cases before they become a crisis.

## Why it matters so much in a merry-go-round

In a [merry-go-round](/learn/what-is-a-merry-go-round), someone who stops paying **after** receiving the pot has effectively taken an interest-free loan from everyone still waiting. In a savings fund, missed dues slowly weaken the fund everyone depends on. Either way, the members who pay on time carry the cost.

## Step 1: Make paying easy and visible

Most "defaults" are forgetfulness or cash-flow timing, not bad faith.

- Send a reminder **two or three days before** the due date, and again on the day. See our [WhatsApp reminder templates](/learn/whatsapp-reminder-templates-for-dues).
- Let people pay by **mobile money** so they can pay from anywhere — [collecting dues with mobile money](/learn/collecting-dues-with-mobile-money).
- Keep a shared record so everyone can see who has paid. Social visibility works.

## Step 2: A private, friendly check-in

If a payment is missed, one person (ideally not the recipient) messages privately: *"Hi Kofi, we didn't see your contribution for March — is everything OK?"* Many problems are solved here, often with a short extension.

## Step 3: Apply the agreed late penalty

A small, automatic late fee — written in your [rules](/learn/merry-go-round-rules-template) — keeps things fair without it being personal. Apply it to everyone, including officials.

## Step 4: Change their standing

After a set number of missed payments, the member's standing changes. A common scheme in savings funds:

| Missed in a row | Standing | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | Active | Full benefits |
| 2 | Behind | No new loans until up to date |
| 3+ | Voided / suspended | Welfare and loans paused; review by the committee |

Standing should **recover automatically** once the member catches up, so there's always a way back. (Fundly applies this for you: when a member pays a missed month, their standing is recalculated straight away.)

## Step 5: Call on the guarantor

For merry-go-round members who've already received the pot, the guarantor named in your rules covers the remaining contributions. This is why [payout order](/learn/merry-go-round-payout-order) and guarantors matter so much — they turn a crisis into an inconvenience.

## Step 6: Agree an exit

If someone can't continue, agree a clean exit: what they're owed or what they owe, a repayment plan, and a date. Record it. A calm exit protects the friendship and the group.

## What not to do

- **Don't shame people in the group chat.** Visibility of a payment list is fine; public call-outs cause people to leave angry.
- **Don't make exceptions for friends or officials.** Inconsistency destroys trust faster than any default.
- **Don't let debts drift.** Unresolved balances at the end of a cycle poison the next one.

## A timeline you can adopt

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| Due date −3 days | Friendly reminder to everyone |
| Due date +2 days | Private check-in |
| Due date +7 days | Late fee applied |
| Second missed month | Standing: behind |
| Third missed month | Standing: suspended; committee review |

## Prevention beats cure

The groups with the fewest problems do three things: they put newer members later in the order, they collect by traceable mobile money, and everyone can see the same up-to-date record.
